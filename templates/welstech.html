WELSTECH Wearable Life Saver Technology — Biometric PPG Pulse Band & Flutter Telemetry

Engineered to deliver continuous, proactive monitoring for individuals with special needs, high-risk patients, and solitary workers. WELSTECH integrates an ultra-compact ESP32-S3 Zero microcontroller with an optical Photoplethysmography (PPG) pulse sensor into a custom SolidWorks 3D-printed enclosure featuring thermal relief ventilation ducts. The device executes real-time analog peak-detection algorithms (calibrated threshold: 852), escalates audible buzzer alarms during cardiac anomalies or unconsciousness, and streams live telemetry to a dedicated Flutter & Dart companion mobile app over local Wi-Fi / BLE protocols.

System Engineering & Embedded Firmware

The Four Pillars of WELSTECH Architecture

How optical biometrics, real-time ESP32 C++ firmware, thermal CAD packaging, and Flutter cross-platform software form an autonomous lifeline.

💓
PILLAR 01 · BIOSENSING

Photoplethysmography (PPG) Sensing

Captures micro-vascular blood volume changes beneath the wrist skin via reflected green/infrared light. The analog signal is sampled at 200 Hz through the ESP32 ADC pin, where software low-pass filters eliminate 50 Hz mains hum and motion artifacts before passing into the systolic peak detector.

  • Analog Threshold: Tuned at 852 ADC units for clean systolic trigger.
  • Filtering: Rolling moving average over 10-second sliding window.
  • Sampling Rate: 200 Hz continuous analog interrupt.
⚡
PILLAR 02 · COMPUTING

ESP32-S3 Zero Dual-Core Architecture

Selected for extreme miniaturization (sub-stamp footprint) while retaining dual-core Xtensa 240 MHz compute. Core 0 manages real-time ADC sampling and buzzer PWM interval timing, while Core 1 handles the lightweight Wi-Fi Access Point HTTP server to prevent network stack jitter from delaying heart rate calculations.

  • Form Factor: ESP32-S3 Zero ultra-miniature package.
  • Local AP Mode: Broadcasts local Wi-Fi hotspot (192.168.4.1).
  • Power Management: Deep sleep duty-cycling for multi-day battery life.
🖨️
PILLAR 03 · MECHANICAL CAD

Thermal-Vented Ergonomic Enclosure

Designed in SolidWorks and 3D printed with heat dissipation venting slots. During initial prototyping, an integrated OLED display trapped ESP32 heat, causing thermal throttling. The architecture was iterated by eliminating the on-board screen, venting the chassis, and delegating visualization entirely to the mobile smartphone app.

  • Tolerance Recalibration: Corrected from 43mm radius to 43mm diameter.
  • Thermal Relief: Integrated passive ventilation slots on side walls.
  • Skin Contact: Ergonomic curved backplate securing sensor optical window.
📱
PILLAR 04 · SOFTWARE & UX

Flutter & Dart Welstech Mobile App

Minimalist mobile companion app engineered for instant visual triage. When unsynced or disconnected, the application window glows with a solid red warning border; upon establishing communication, it switches to a reassuring blue. Renders real-time BPM graphs, logs emergency timestamps, and triggers automated SOS SMS alerts.

  • Visual Triage: Dynamic border (Red = Unsynced, Blue = Synced).
  • Data Ingestion: Continuous HTTP client requests every 200–500 ms.
  • Emergency SOS: Background contact broadcast when threshold tripped.

Prototyping & Iteration Journal

Transparent Hardware Iteration & Failures Overcome

Real engineering involves honest mistakes, thermal discoveries, and iterative design refinement.

01
Thermal Discovery

Overcoming ESP32 Heat Entrapment & Display Removal

Initial designs placed a 0.96" I2C OLED display directly above the ESP32. In close proximity, the ESP32's Wi-Fi transmission heated the enclosure, causing the OLED panel to malfunction and raising skin contact temperatures. Rather than adding active fans, we made the architectural decision to remove the OLED display entirely, venting the 3D-printed chassis, and streaming data over Wi-Fi/BLE to the mobile phone.

02
Mechanical CAD

The 43 mm Radius vs. Diameter SolidWorks Lesson

During early CAD modeling, the measured 43 mm wristband diameter was mistakenly inputted as a radius parameter in SolidWorks. The resulting 3D print was double the intended size (86 mm), dwarfing a real commercial watch. Taking this as an engineering lesson, the model was completely rescaled, adding ergonomic wrist curvature, tolerance offsets, and integrated strap retaining loops.

03
Firmware Tuning

Tuning the Analog 852 Systolic Peak Threshold

Raw ADC readings fluctuate based on ambient light, skin tone, and wrist strap tightness. Through extensive bench testing across multiple subjects, the optimal Schmitt-trigger threshold of 852 was determined, yielding stable 96–100 BPM rolling averages without ghost triggers or missed systolic peaks.
