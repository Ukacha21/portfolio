Global Diagnostic Gap

The Problem: Decay is Invisible Until Children Present in Pain

In low-resource communities and rural clinics, tooth decay is undetected during the critical early window when it is 100% reversible without drilling.

The Reversible Window Lost to Invisibility

Enamel demineralization is caused by acid-producing bacteria (such as Streptococcus mutans). In its earliest phase—ICDAS Stage 1 and 2—mineral loss occurs beneath an intact outer surface layer (the classic "white spot lesion").

At this stage, no drilling or filling is required: topical fluoride varnish and remineralization therapy can fully repair the mineral lattice. However, the human eye cannot detect sub-surface demineralization in ambient light. By the time a visible dark hole appears or a child complains of pain, the enamel has collapsed into dentin, mandating invasive drilling, costly restorations, or complete tooth extraction.

“Oral diseases affect nearly 3.5 billion people worldwide. Severe periodontal disease and untreated dental caries in deciduous teeth affect over 500 million children. Lack of accessible screening equipment in primary care remains the fundamental barrier.”

— World Health Organization (WHO) Global Oral Health Action Plan 2023–2030

Why Existing Diagnostic Technologies Cannot Scale

  • ✕
    Bitewing Radiography ($20–150 / scan + $15,000 equipment): Requires heavy mains electricity, lead-lined radiation shielding, darkrooms, and a licensed radiologist. Unusable in rural village schools and subjects children to ionizing radiation.
  • ✕
    Clinical Fluorescence Devices ($2,000–$4,500 DIAGNOdent): Expensive laser devices designed exclusively for high-end dental surgeries. Require delicate optical fiber tips, frequent recalibration, and specialized dental clinician operation.
  • ✕
    Visual-Tactile Probing (Sharp Explorer): Outdated sharp metal dental explorer hooks can physically punch through weakened demineralized enamel, inadvertently converting a reversible lesion into an irreversible physical cavity.

Dual-Wavelength Biophysics

01
405 nm Quantitative Light-Induced Fluorescence (QLF):

Healthy enamel hydroxyapatite absorbs 405 nm violet light and emits strong green autofluorescence centered at 515 nm. Demineralized lesions lose mineral density, increasing light scattering and decreasing green emission by 10.9% to 59.9%. Concurrently, bacterial protoporphyrin IX emits fluorescent red light at 630 nm.

02
850 nm Near-Infrared Transillumination (NILT):

Enamel is highly transparent to 850 nm near-infrared light, while decayed tooth structure scatters and blocks NIR photons. This illuminates early interproximal caries between adjacent teeth that surface cameras miss entirely.

03
The Filterless Silicon Solution (ams OSRAM AS7341):

Conventional QLF systems require a precision optical longpass filter glass (£50–£200) to block 405 nm excitation bounce. The AS7341 integrates 11 narrowband Fabry-Pérot dielectric interference filters directly onto silicon photodiodes, measuring 415nm to 680nm in parallel at under £4.00 total chip cost.

System Mechatronics

4-Pillar Hardware Architecture

Engineered from bare components for sub-£20 volume production and extreme durability in rural field work.

01
💡

Dual-Wavelength Solid-State Emitter

Narrow-angle Cree 405 nm violet LED (15° viewing angle) for intense fluorophore excitation combined with a Vishay 850 nm infrared LED for transillumination, driven by a precision constant-current driver.

  • Excitation Peak: 405 nm ± 5 nm FWHM (Violet QLF)
  • NIR Transillumination: 850 nm high-power infrared
  • Eye Safety: IEC 62471 photobiological safety compliant
02
🔬

AS7341 11-Channel Spectral Sensor

Silicon CMOS multi-spectral sensor with 8 visible channels (415–680 nm), clear channel, flicker detection, and NIR. Connects via standard I2C bus at 400 kHz to read complete spectral distribution in under 200 ms.

  • ADC Resolution: 16-bit independent dual-channel integration
  • Direct Deposition: Nano-optic dielectric filters on silicon
  • Dynamic Range: Up to 100,000 lux ambient rejection
03
⚡

ESP32-S3 Zero Dual-Core MCU

Dual-core Xtensa 32-bit LX7 processor running at 240 MHz. Executes the on-board spectral ratio classification algorithm in real-time while broadcasting diagnostic packets over Bluetooth 5.0 (BLE) and Wi-Fi.

  • Wireless: BLE 5.0 mesh + 802.11b/g/n Wi-Fi
  • Tele-Dentistry: Instant sync to CHW mobile app or cloud dashboard
  • Form Factor: Ultra-compact 23 mm × 18 mm footprint
04
🔋

Ergonomic Housing & 18650 Power

19 mm diameter cylindrical ABS barrel mimicking standard dental mirrors. Powered by a standard protected 18650 Li-ion cell providing 8+ hours continuous screening (over 1,200 tooth scans) with USB-C TP4056 charging.

  • Battery: 2,000 mAh 18650 cell (USB-C rechargeable)
  • Shielding: Autoclavable silicone optical cone (35 mm)
  • Weight: Under 95 grams with battery installed

Verification & Empirical Testing

James Dyson Award Prototype Iteration Log

Verifiable physical progression from breadboard spectral validation to connected tele-dentistry prototype.

ITERATION 01

AS7341 I2C Driver & Spectral Calibration on ESP32

Interfaced the ams OSRAM AS7341 breakout board to an ESP32-S3 over I2C (400 kHz). Tested baseline sensitivity across the 8 visible spectral bands (F1 415nm to F8 680nm) under controlled illumination. Confirmed 16-bit ADC integration and dynamic gain control.

Validation: Verified sub-200ms read times with clear channel ambient light quantification.
ITERATION 02

405 nm QLF Excitation on Enamel Proxies & Filterless Detection

Illuminated intact white acrylic tooth proxies and localized demineralized mock lesions with a 405 nm violet LED. Measured channel differential between healthy areas and simulated decay. Developed the Red/Green (F7/F4) ratio algorithm in firmware.

Breakthrough: Successfully demonstrated that silicon interference filters reject 405 nm bounce without requiring external optical longpass filter glass.
ITERATION 03

Ambient Light Shielding Cap & 3-Color Traffic Light Output

Designed and 3D printed a conical 35 mm shielding hood to seat securely against the dental crown, eliminating solar and fluorescent room light artifact. Implemented the RGB traffic-light ring (Green = Healthy, Amber = Early Lesion, Red = Active Cavity).

Ergonomics: Non-clinical operators achieved 100% agreement on diagnostic state in under 3 seconds per tooth.
ITERATION 04

BLE 5.0 Companion Tele-Dentistry Architecture & Clinical Pilot

Implemented Bluetooth Low Energy GATT service transmitting timestamped spectral channel values and diagnostic classifications to a paired mobile app. Outlined pilot study with rural community health clinics in Kenya and Turkey.

Next Phase: CE / FDA Class I medical screening device notification and multicenter clinical trial.
