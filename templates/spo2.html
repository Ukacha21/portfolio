From Schematics & Custom Footprints to Real Manufacturer Clearance Tolerances in KiCad 10.0

By Ukacha JUMA — Mechatronics Engineer & Hardware Developer

Engineered as a comprehensive hands-on milestone in precision Printed Circuit Board (PCB) Design using KiCad 10.0. Moving past abstract circuit theory, this project encompasses the complete physical realization workflow: drawing custom schematic symbols, crafting manufacturer-compliant footprints from scratch, component selection, netlist assignment, and executing 2-layer routing with 45° trace chamfers and dedicated ground planes. 01

Schematic Capture & Custom Symbol Creation

Drafted multi-device electronic schematic in KiCad Eeschema. Created bespoke KiCad symbols for multi-pin breakout modules where standard libraries lacked appropriate pinouts, binding electrical ERC nets.

02

Footprint Engineering from Datasheet Geometries

Constructed precision footprints from scratch using vendor mechanical drawings: defining exact pad diameters (0.8–1.0 mm drill, 1.6–1.8 mm copper annular ring), pin pitches (2.54 mm headers, 6mm tactile switches), and courtyards.

03

Component Selection & Real-World Parametrics

Evaluated passive tolerances (1% metal film 1kΩ resistors, 50V X7R ceramic capacitors) and physical packaging clearances to prevent mechanical interference between the Arduino Nano, OLED display, and human tactile interface.

04

2-Layer Routing, Bus Integrity & Chamfers

Routed all 31 signal nets on F.Cu (Front Copper) and power/ground distribution on B.Cu (Back Copper). Enforced strict 45-degree angle trace corners to prevent RF reflections and acid traps during chemical etching.

05

Design Rule Checking (DRC) & Fabrication Readiness

Configured manufacturer-specific DRC clearance matrices (track-to-track 0.2mm, drill-to-copper 0.3mm, minimum annular ring). Resolved all 31 nets with zero unrouted tracks, generating verified gerber & drill files.

Hardware Architecture

The Four Pillars of the SPO2 PCB Design

An engineering breakdown of component selection, physical trace routing, debouncing mathematics, and manufacturing constraints.

🔴
PILLAR 01

Optical Biosensing & MAX30102 Integration

The MAX30102 operates via dual-wavelength photoplethysmography (PPG), emitting alternating pulses of 660 nm Red light (absorbed primarily by deoxygenated hemoglobin) and 880 nm Infrared light (absorbed by oxygenated hemoglobin). The ratio of normalized AC to DC signal components computes arterial oxygen saturation:

R = (AC_red / DC_red) / (AC_ir / DC_ir) → SpO2 = 110 - 25 × R

The PCB provides short, direct I2C SDA and SCL routing from the sensor header to the microcontroller, minimizing parasitic capacitance on the serial clock line.

⚡
PILLAR 02

ATmega328P Core & Dual-Rail Routing

The board utilizes an Arduino Nano v3 socket as the primary compute module, hosting an ATmega328P processor at 16 MHz. Power distribution is separated into:

  • +5V Bus (B.Cu Back Copper): Low-impedance supply routed with wide copper paths to handle LED peak pulse currents.
  • Signal Plane (F.Cu Front Copper): High-impedance digital I2C and interrupt signals isolated from switching noise.
  • Ground Return: Minimized loop area around bypass capacitors to maintain high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).
🎛️
PILLAR 03

Hardware RC Pushbutton Debouncing

Mechanical switches generate 5–20 ms of contact bounce when pressed, causing false multiple triggers in digital interrupt routines. Instead of wasting MCU timer cycles on software delays, the SPO2 board embeds dedicated analog RC low-pass debouncing networks for SW1_MENU, SW2_UP, and SW3_DOWN:

τ = R × C = 1,000 Ω × 100 × 10⁻⁹ F = 0.10 ms (100 µs)

The RC circuit suppresses high-frequency contact bounce spikes while maintaining crisp responsiveness for UI interaction.

📐
PILLAR 04

Manufacturing DRC & KiCad 10.0 Rules

Before locking measurements, real-world manufacturing constraints from commercial PCB fabrication facilities (e.g. JLCPCB / PCBWay) were applied to KiCad's Design Rules Checker:

  • Minimum Track Width: 0.4000 mm (15.75 mils) for standard signals; 0.6000 mm for power rails.
  • Electrical Clearance: 0.2000 mm (7.87 mils) net-to-net clearance.
  • Through-Hole Drill & Annular Ring: 0.8 mm minimum hole with 0.4 mm copper ring.
  • 45-Degree Chamfer Routing: Zero 90-degree acid-trap corners across all 31 routed nets.
