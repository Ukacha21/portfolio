THE PROBLEM

The Synthetic Fibre Crisis in Domestic Laundry

Why residential washing machines are the single largest primary source of ocean microplastics — and how current solutions fail.

IUCN & NIH EVIDENCE

33% of Marine Microplastics Come from Synthetic Clothing

According to landmark IUCN (2017) research reaffirmed by 2024 NIH oceanographic audits, approximately 33% of all primary microplastics entering marine ecosystems originate from synthetic textiles washed at home.

A single polyester fleece releases up to 250,000 synthetic fibres per wash cycle. Because these fibres are microscopic (10–100 µm diameter), standard municipal wastewater treatment plants capture only ~70% — allowing hundreds of billions of indestructible polymer threads to discharge into rivers and oceans daily.

🩸 Microplastics are now verified in 80% of healthy human blood donors (Vethaak & Legler, 2021) and deep lung tissue.
THE MARKET GAP

Existing Products Demand Too Much Effort & Money

Current market solutions suffer from severe adoption barriers:

  • Wall-Mounted External Units (€60–€350): Require wall drilling, external plumbing modifications, permanent laundry room footprint, and proprietary disposable cartridges.
  • In-Drum Laundry Bags: Require manual sorting for every wash, capture fewer than 55% of fibres, and wear out rapidly.
  • The "Wet Mesh" Disgust Barrier: Every existing filter forces users to manually scrape or rinse a soggy, smelly mesh of wet lint — leading to abandoned maintenance.
🎯 The FLOWGUARD Goal: Sub-£12 manufacturing cost, tool-free snap-on install in <10s, and dry pellet compaction with zero wet mesh contact.

THE SOLUTION

The FLOWGUARD Four-Pillar Architecture

How civil engineering sedimentation principles and precision mechanical compaction merge into a sub-£12 consumer device.

01
⚡

Stepped Tool-Free Snap-On Clamp

Engineered with a dual-step inner bore compatible with standard 19 mm and 22 mm drain hoses. Dual finger-tabs provide an audible, tactile click upon seating with zero torque tools, zero hose cutting, and zero risk of wall damage.

Material: High-Impact Polypropylene (PP) · Audible Click Lock
02
🌀

Velocity-Reduction Bypass Chamber

Based on civil engineering sand-trap sedimentation physics: suddenly expanding the flow diameter drops water velocity from 1.85 m/s to 0.22 m/s. Wetted synthetic fibres precipitate out of suspension onto a 100 µm 316 stainless mesh with negligible backpressure.

Δv: -88% Velocity Drop · Backpressure < 0.04 bar
03
🔩

Single-Action Twist-Piston Compactor

A single turn of the ergonomic rear threaded cap drives a precision Delrin/PP piston across the mesh chamber. Squeezes excess water through the mesh and ejects a compacted, dry solid pellet directly into the recycling or waste bin.

Compaction: 12:1 Volumetric Ratio · Zero Wet Contact
04
♻️

Closed-Loop Textile Circularity

Because laundry microfibres are monomaterial synthetic polymers (primarily polyester and nylon), the compacted dry pellets are clean, uniform, and ideal for thermal reprocessing into recycled plastic feedstock rather than landfilling.

Pure Monomaterial Polymer · Ready for Reprocessing
DESIGN EVOLUTION

Iterative Prototyping & Failure Documentation

Real engineering requires verifiable failure analysis. How four prototype iterations forged the final design.

ITERATION 01 — FAILED

In-Line Mesh Sleeve Across Full Hose Cross-Section

The Concept: Modelled a simple 100 µm stainless cylinder directly inside the hose stream in SolidWorks.
The Failure: Flow simulation and physical tap testing revealed that full-bore mesh generated over 0.35 bar backpressure within 2 minutes of fibre accumulation. This backpressure trips residential washing machine drain pump fault sensors, aborting the cycle.
The Pivot: Discarded inline full-bore filtering; invented the velocity-reduction bypass chamber.

ITERATION 02 — FAILED

Quarter-Turn Bayonet Lock on Exterior Drain Hose

The Concept: A rotating bayonet fitting designed for rapid attachment to drain hoses.
The Failure: Due to variance in ribbed PVC drain hose wall thickness across brands (Bosch, Samsung, Beko), the bayonet slipped under thermal cycling at 60°C wash water.
The Pivot: Switched to dual finger-tab spring latches with stepped inner silicone seals that grip the hose ridges mechanically.

ITERATION 03 — VALIDATED

Sedimentation Bypass Chamber with FreeRTOS Backwash Sensor

The Result: Expanding the housing cross-section plunged water velocity by 88%. Denser-than-water fibres precipitated down into the trap while main effluent flowed freely. SolidWorks Flow Simulation confirmed 94.2% microfibre retention with backpressure below 0.04 bar.

ITERATION 04 — FINAL FORM

Integrated Twist-Piston Pellet Compactor

The Breakthrough: Converted the maintenance experience from messy wet lint extraction to a clean, tactile twist that ejects dry pellets. Physical PLA 3D printed prototype tested with fleece fabric slurry successfully extruded solid pellets.
