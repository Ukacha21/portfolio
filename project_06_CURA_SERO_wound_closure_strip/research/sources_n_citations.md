## 3. Gecko-Inspired Biomimetic Adhesion — Science Basis

### 3.1 Gecko Adhesion Mechanism

| Fact | Figure | Source | Use In |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gecko adhesion mechanism | **Van der Waals forces** generated by hierarchical micro-structures (setae → spatulae) maximising contact area | Autumn, K. et al. (2002). *Evidence for van der Waals adhesion in gecko setae.* PNAS, 99(19), 12252–12256. | Core design principle |
| Gecko adhesion on wet surfaces | Gecko setae maintain adhesion on wet glass; force reduced but functional | Stark, A. Y. et al. (2012). *The role of surface chemistry in gecko adhesion.* Journal of Experimental Biology. | Wet-surface adhesion claim |
| Gecko-inspired dry adhesive — biomedical application | PDMS micropillar arrays adapted for wound dressing; tissue trauma reduced vs chemical adhesives | Cho, W. K. et al. (2019). *Microstructured surgical bandages demonstrate excellent adhesive properties.* Advanced Functional Materials. | Clinical adaptation |
| Biodegradable gecko-inspired adhesive | Biodegradable PLGA-based micropillar arrays for transient wound closure | Bettinger, C. J. et al. (2009). *Biocompatible biodegradable polymer adhesives.* Biomaterials. | Future section — biodegradable version |

**Key quote (Autumn et al., 2002, PNAS):**
> *"The adhesive performance of gecko setae is dominated by van der Waals forces, not molecular adhesion or suction — a finding with profound implications for engineered dry adhesives."*

### 3.2 Micropillar Array Design Parameters

| Parameter | Specification | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Pillar diameter | **10–50 µm** | Optimised from gecko seta research; Cho et al. (2019); Autumn et al. (2002) |
| Pillar tip geometry | **Mushroom-cap** (flat-top) — maximises van der Waals contact area | Gorb, S. N. et al. (2007). *Mushroom-shaped fibrillar adhesive microstructure.* Bioinspiration & Biomimetics. |
| Array pattern | **Hexagonal** — maximises density | Standard from PDMS micropillar literature |
| Substrate material | **Medical-grade silicone (USP Class VI)** — biocompatible, flexible, ISO 10993-5 tested | ISO 10993-5:2009. *Biological evaluation of medical devices.* |
| Gradient modulus advantage | Stiffer tips / softer base — improves adhesion on rough biological surfaces | Kim, S. et al. (2021). *Gradient micropillar arrays for enhanced wet adhesion.* NIH / PubMed, PMC. |

## 8. Application-Ready Quotes & Phrasing

### For "The Problem":
> *"In rural sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia, the nearest surgical facility may be hours or days away. A laceration that requires sutures — a 10-minute procedure in a clinic — leads to infection and permanent scarring from an entirely treatable injury. For 2 billion people, sutures are not inaccessible — they are simply unreachable."*
> — Source: Meara et al. (2015), The Lancet.

### For "How It Works" — gecko adhesion:
> *"The hexagonal micropillar array generates van der Waals adhesion at microscopic scale — the same physical mechanism that allows geckos to cling to wet glass — creating secure bonding on sweaty, dirty, or bleeding skin without any chemical adhesive."*
> — Source: Autumn et al. (2002), PNAS.

### For "What Sets It Apart":
> *"AVICENNA is the first closure system combining wet-surface micro-suction adhesion, active mechanical wound-edge tension, and 48–72 hour slow-release antimicrobial delivery in a single sealed unit. No existing product addresses all three failure modes simultaneously."*
