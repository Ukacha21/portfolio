# Project 06 — AVICENNA: Wet-Surface Wound Closure Strip

**James Dyson Award 2026**
**Category:** Medical Devices / Biomimetic Engineering
**Scope:** Regional focus: Rural Sub-Saharan Africa, Southeast Asia

## The Problem

In rural areas, lacerations requiring sutures go untreated. Standard closure strips fail on sweaty or dirty skin and cannot hold tension across deep wounds — leading to infection and scarring.

## The Solution

A closure strip with a bio-inspired micro-suction surface (gecko adhesion) that bonds to wet and dirty skin, applies suture-equivalent tension, and includes a slow-release antimicrobial layer. Packaged for community health worker use.

### ❓ Where I got my inspiration
*(Limit: 650 characters)*

In rural sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia, lacerations often go untreated — the nearest surgical facility is hours away and community health workers lack suturing skills. Standard closure strips fail immediately on sweaty or wet skin and cannot hold tension across deep wounds. The result: infection and scarring from entirely treatable injuries.

The gecko's ability to grip wet surfaces via micro-suction — without any adhesive — offered a model for a closure strip that works where adhesives fail. Combining this with a tension mechanism and antimicrobial release created a self-contained system requiring no clinical training.

---

### ❓ How it works
*(Limit: 975 characters)*

The strip has three functional layers. The base is a biocompatible silicone substrate moulded with a micro-pillar array — hexagonal pillars 10–50 µm in diameter — that creates van der Waals adhesion against wet tissue surfaces. Unlike chemical adhesives, this suction mechanism grips sweaty, dirty, or bleeding skin by maximising contact area at microscopic scale.

The middle layer is a mechanical tension chassis: two rigid polymer rails connected by a ratcheting tensioner that draws wound edges together after the strip is applied. The health worker advances the ratchet until tissue edges are approximated, providing closure tension equivalent to a 3-0 suture.

The outer layer is a hydrogel matrix loaded with slow-release chlorhexidine gluconate. As wound exudate contacts the hydrogel, antimicrobial agent is released at a controlled rate over 48–72 hours, maintaining a sterile wound environment throughout the critical early healing phase.

---

### ❓ How you developed your design | Design Process
*(Limit: 1300 characters)*

The design process began with a clinical gap analysis. The target user — a rural health worker with no surgical training — needed a solution applicable in under 60 seconds, effective on wet and contaminated skin, strong enough to hold a deep laceration, and protective against infection.

The first design question was adhesion mechanism. Standard acrylic and silicone pressure-sensitive adhesives were tested against wet skin simulations and failed within minutes — consistent with known clinical literature. Cyanoacrylate tissue glues were eliminated due to toxicity concerns and their need for dry surfaces. Bioinspired micro-suction, modelled on gecko setae, was identified as the only mechanism capable of wet-surface bonding without chemical adhesion.

Micro-pillar geometry was optimised computationally: hexagonal array at 10–50 µm pitch with mushroom-cap tip profiles, maximising van der Waals contact on irregular biological surfaces. Biocompatible medical-grade silicone (USP Class VI) was selected as substrate.

The tension mechanism was designed for one-handed operation: a ratchet that advances in one direction and cannot overtighten, preventing the excessive force that causes tissue necrosis. Antimicrobial selection prioritised broad-spectrum coverage with low resistance risk — chlorhexidine gluconate at 0.5% slow-release from a PVA hydrogel matrix.

---

### ❓ What sets it apart
*(Limit: 975 characters)*

Standard wound closure strips (Steri-Strip, Dermabond) fail on wet, sweaty, or contaminated skin — the exact conditions present in rural and field settings. Sutures require sterile technique, instruments, trained practitioners, and anaesthetic. Neither option is accessible in last-mile healthcare.

AVICENNA is the first closure strip combining wet-surface micro-suction adhesion, active mechanical tension, and integrated antimicrobial release in a single unit. Competitors address one need only: adhesive strips handle dry wounds; tissue glues cannot manage tension; antimicrobial dressings are passive. AVICENNA integrates all three in a format requiring no training beyond basic first aid.

Packaged as a single sealed unit requiring no preparation, no sterile field, and no instruments, it can be deployed by anyone — extending surgical-quality wound closure to the 2 billion people without access to a clinic.

### ❓ What the future holds for it
*(Limit: 650 characters)*

Clinical validation will begin with a prospective study in partnership with a rural health organisation, measuring closure strength, infection rate, and healing outcomes against standard field dressings. Regulatory filing will target WHO prequalification and CE marking as a Class IIa device. A packaging redesign will produce single-sterile units compatible with community health worker resupply chains. Long-term development will explore biodegradable micro-pillar substrates to eliminate strip removal, allowing the device to absorb as the wound heals.
